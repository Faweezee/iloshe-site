**Introduction**

Fake or defective land titles are one of the most common pitfalls in property transactions. Sellers often present unverified or fraudulent documents such as invalid survey plans, fake Certificates of Occupancy (C of O), or even attempt to sell family land without proper authorization.

These practices not only jeopardize buyers financially but also expose them to long-term legal disputes.

**The Nature of Fake Titles**

**Invalid survey plans:** Some sellers present survey documents that do not correspond to the actual land being sold. These plans may be forged or copied from unrelated properties.

**Fake Certificates of Occupancy:** The C of O is one of the most important documents in Nigerian property law, signifying government approval of ownership. Fraudsters often forge these documents to deceive unsuspecting buyers.

**Unauthorized family land sales:** In many communities, family land cannot be sold without the consent of all principal members. Unscrupulous individuals sometimes sell such land without approval, leading to disputes.

**Why Buyers Fall VictimIgnorance of property law:** Many buyers lack adequate knowledge of the legal requirements for land ownership.Failure to conduct due diligence: Buyers often skip verification processes, relying solely on the seller’s word.Pressure to secure land quickly: The fear of losing out on a “good deal” makes buyers overlook critical checks.

**Consequences of Fake** 

**TitlesFinancial loss:** Buyers may lose their entire investment if the land is reclaimed by rightful owners or the government.Legal battles: Court cases over land ownership can drag on for years, draining resources.

**Loss of development opportunities:** Without valid titles, buyers cannot obtain building permits or loans, stalling development plans.How to Detect Fake or Defective TitlesVerify documents with authorities: Always cross-check survey plans and C of O with the Lands Registry.

**Engage professional surveyors:** Licensed surveyors can confirm whether the land matches the survey plan.Consult legal experts: Lawyers specializing in property law can identify inconsistencies in documents.Community verification: In cases of family land, consult community leaders or family heads to confirm authorization.

**Preventive Measures for BuyersConduct thorough due diligence:** Never rely solely on seller-provided documents.Insist on original documents: Photocopies can easily be forged; always demand originals.Obtain government consent: For certain transactions, government approval is mandatory.

**Use escrow arrangements:** Payments can be held in escrow until all documents are verified.ConclusionFake or defective titles remain a significant threat in real estate transactions. Buyers must prioritize verification, legal consultation, and due diligence to avoid falling prey to fraudulent sellers. The cost of caution is far less than the financial and emotional toll of losing property to invalid documents.
