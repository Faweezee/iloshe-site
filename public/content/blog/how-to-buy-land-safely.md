Buying land in Nigeria is one of the most empowering financial moves an individual or family can make. However, fear of land grabbers ("Omonile"), double allocations, and government acquisitions stops many buyers from taking the leap.

At Iloshe Properties, we believe that land buying should be transparent and risk-free. Here is our essential step-by-step checklist to ensure your investment is secure:

### Step 1: Demand the Survey Plan & Charting Information
Before paying any deposit, request a copy of the estate's official survey plan. Take the coordinates on the plan to the Lagos State Surveyor General's Office (or state land registry) to "chart" the land. Charting confirms whether the land is free from government acquisition.

### Step 2: Verify the Title Type
Not all land documents offer the same legal protection. Understand what title your property holds:
- **Certificate of Occupancy (C of O):** The highest title issued directly by the state government, valid for 99 years.
- **Governor's Consent:** Approval granted by the Governor when land with an existing C of O or Gazette is resold.
- **Excision & Gazette:** Land that has been formally released by government back to indigenous communities and gazetted.

### Step 3: Conduct Physical Pegging & Allocation Inspection
Never complete land payments without physical plot allocation. Ensure the estate developer pegs your exact plot boundaries on-site in your presence, and issues an official Deed of Assignment.
